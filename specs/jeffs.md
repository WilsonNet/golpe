# Jeffs, the Executioner

**Intent:** a point-blank executioner and a smoke-and-mirrors schemer. Where Lia
reads and guards and Anands takes the initiative, Jeffs *ambushes*: the shotgun
is the closest weapon in the game — lethal at touch, useless at range — and the
smoke grenade is what lets him decide the distance. He is the sword's oldest
trick — the blade — in the coat of a hired gun.

The visual reference is a middle-aged man with a fancy trench coat: greying
slicked-back hair, a weathered face, a dark charcoal coat with gold buttons, a
white shirt and a red tie, the collar up. The sprite sheet (`assets/jeffs.png`)
is the same nine-frame strip as the other heroes' (see [anands.md](anands.md)
for the layout contract), generated by `scripts/make-jeffs-art.py`, and the hit
poses are derived from it in code exactly like the others — a human artist
replaces the sheet, not the machinery.

## The sword (melee stance)

Jeffs carries **the sword, unchanged** — `MELEE_WEAPONS.sword`: the slash
chain, the uppercut, the block, the Massive Strike. The blade is the kit's
honest answer at any range where the shotgun has no business; it is the same
frame data as Lia's, because a blade that changed its timing per wielder would
be two weapons wearing one name. The double-tap dash is the sword's.

## The shotgun (gun stance)

Reaper's Hellfire Shotguns, tuned to this game's scale: a slow, lethal,
cone-spread burst.

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| Pellets | 6 |
| Cooldown | 900ms (vs the pistol's 250) |
| Damage per pellet | 17 (102 if all six land) |
| Pellet speed | 900 px/s |
| Spread | ±10°, six fixed angles |

- **The delay is the whole weapon.** Nine hundred milliseconds between shots
  is nearly four pistol shots and over eight machine-gun rounds — a missed
  blast is a window the room can walk through. The shotgun is a *commitment*
  weapon, like the Massive: you fire when you are sure, or you pay.
- **Point blank is lethal.** Six pellets at touching range all land: 102
  damage, a one-shot kill against a full bar. The weapon's entire identity is
  in that number — a shotgun that needed two blasts at touching range would be
  a slow pistol.
- **The spread is the range.** The cone is fixed at the muzzle, so distance
  *is* the miss: at 60px every pellet connects; at 200px the edge pellets are
  off a 32px body and only the middle lands; at 400px the blast is a warning
  shot. There is no damage falloff — the cone already is the falloff, and a
  pellet that lands at any distance hits for the same 17.
- **Pellets are bullets.** Each is an ordinary server-owned bullet: blocked
  by a front guard, stopped by geometry, dead-reckoned by the client. Six
  pellets into a raised guard is six absorbed shots.
- **The fan is deterministic.** The six angles are fixed (`-10°…+10°` in even
  steps) — no randomness, so both sides spawn the same pattern from the same
  aim and the client's prediction never disagrees with the server's.
- **Feeds the meter like any bullet.** The shotgun is an ordinary weapon; a
  point-blank kill pays the ultimate.

## The smoke grenade (item)

A thrown canister that pops into a **vision cloud** — Counter-Strike's smoke
at this game's scale, and the trap's structural cousin: a server-placed world
object that travels in the snapshot in full, single-use, and is drawn per the
side that is looking at it.

- **Two per life.** The smoke hides — it does not hurt — so it gets the
  grenade's two charges, not the trap's three. Charges reset on respawn and
  round reset; a dead Jeffs' clouds leave the floor with him.
- **Thrown, then planted.** The canister arcs like the HE grenade (700 px/s,
  gravity 900, 0.4 restitution bounce) and its fuse runs out after 900ms;
  where it stops, the cloud blooms. A canister that is still rolling when the
  fuse ends blooms where it is — smoking mid-air is a real throw.
- **The cloud:** 150px radius, lasting 6.5s, anchored where it bloomed.
- **Allies see through it.** Your own smoke and every teammate's is drawn at a
  fraction of full opacity — a near-transparent haze that reads as "your
  smoke" without hiding the fight behind it. The side a cloud belongs to is
  the same `sameTeam` predicate the friendly-fade of traps uses.
- **Enemies see a wall.** A hostile cloud is full-strength: layered, drifting
  puffs of grey that read as *something is in there* and hide exactly what. It
  is the point of the item — the enemy cannot count you, cannot see whether
  you are still there, cannot read how many walked in.
- **Ally smoke hides the people inside.** A fighter standing inside a cloud
  that belongs to *their own side* is invisible to everyone hostile to them:
  no sprite, no shadow, no nameplate, no health bar. "Who is in the smoke" is
  the question the cloud exists to make unanswerable — the thrower can walk
  into their own smoke to vanish, and a teammate caught mid-retreat can be
  swallowed by a friendly cloud. The local fighter is never hidden from
  themselves.
- **Enemy smoke hides nobody.** Walking into a cloud the *enemy* threw does
  not conceal you — you are in their smoke, their side can see you plainly,
  and your own side sees you too. The concealment belongs to the smoke's
  owner, which is the whole trade of the item: it is a *friendly* tool, not a
  neutral one.
- **It stops nothing.** No damage, no bullets blocked, no movement. The smoke
  is pure vision — the trap locks, the smoke *lies*.
- The one-shot visual (the canister's bloom puff) is client-side, edge-detected
  on new cloud ids like the singularity's detonation; a dropped datagram costs
  a puff, never the concealment, which is re-derived from the cloud list every
  snapshot.

## The Death Blossom (ultimate)

Reaper's Death Blossom, at this game's scale: the caster stops, and for two
seconds the world around them is a storm of gunfire. **Every ultimate gets the
announcement** — the same 1100ms freeze and portrait card the black hole has.

- **Casting:** hold R to aim. There is nothing to aim — the preview is a
  pulsing **ring at the blast radius** around the caster, because for a radial
  ultimate the radius is the information. Release to cast.
- **The channel:** after the freeze, the caster spins in place for **2000ms**.
  Walk speed is halved and dash, jump, block, stance and the sword are all
  unavailable for the whole channel — the storm is a commitment, exactly like
  Reaper's. Gravity and collision are ordinary: the caster can be carried, can
  be shot, can be sworded, and falls off ledges while spinning.
- **The storm:** every **250ms**, every enemy within **260px** of the caster
  with **line of sight** takes **13 damage** — 104 over the full channel, a
  full bar. A foe who stands in the storm dies; a foe who dashes out of the
  ring, or ducks behind a platform, or knocks the caster down, lives.
- **It pays nobody.** The storm feeds no meter and no Play of the Game burst —
  the ultimate is the one weapon that cannot feed itself, exactly like the
  hole and the dragon.
- **No friendly fire.** Allies walk through the storm untouched — the one
  `hostile` predicate, as always.
- **The one interrupt: a knockdown.** The channel is not stopped by ordinary
  hitstun — a slash's 190ms is a blink inside two seconds — but the big CC
  ends it instantly: the chain's finisher, the thrust, the shoryuken, a
  Massive's blast. That is the counterplay, and it is why the storm reads as a
  threat rather than a win button: the answer is the same read the sword has
  been teaching since the first match — commit the knockdown, or die.
- **The spin is simulated, the damage is not.** `blossomTimer` rides in
  `PlayerPosition` and both sides tick it — a client predicts its own spin
  exactly as it predicts a dash — while the interval damage is the server's
  alone, like the black hole's ticks. The whole channel (the walk slow, the
  gates, the interrupt) is shared code; only the hurt is the server's.
- **The draw:** a spinning storm — the caster's sprite rotated at full spin,
  radial streaks of gunfire, muzzle flashes circling, shell casings arcing
  away, smoke and sparks — the heaviest particle budget in the game, because
  the ultimate is the one moment the room is allowed to be loud.

## The AI

`EnemyBrain` picks Jeffs' modules: the **sword's** `MeleeBrain` (the blade is
the blade), a new **BlossomBrain** for the ultimate and a smoke behaviour in
the coordinator.

- **BlossomBrain** holds the button when the storm pays: two or more foes
  within ~240px of the caster, a finisher inside the ring, an outnumbered
  fight, or a meter held ready for ten seconds (the patience rule the hole
  shares). It never overrides the aim — there is nothing to aim.
- **The shotgun is a range preference, not a new brain.** The bot fights the
  sword game and fires the shotgun where the shotgun works: it closes to
  ~110px before pulling the trigger, instead of the gun's 80px kite — the
  coordinator's `GUN_KITE_RANGE_PX` becomes the shotgun's settle range for
  this hero. Bots miss at range exactly like players, because the cone is
  real.
- **The smoke is a panic button.** The coordinator throws it at its own feet
  when hurt (under 40 HP) with a foe inside ~320px, or when outnumbered — the
  cloud is ally smoke, so the bot vanishes from the enemy's screen while
  still seeing them. The aim for the throw is straight down.

## The art budget

`scripts/make-jeffs-art.py` generates the sheet a human artist must replace:

| Sheet | Cells | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `jeffs.png` | 9 x 32x48 | 0-3 walk left, 4 face-on, 5-8 walk right |
| `jeffs-roll.png` | 16 x 40x48 | 0-7 roll right, 8-15 roll left (mirrors) |

The hit poses are derived from the face-on frame in code (`createHeroPoses`),
exactly like the other heroes' — the placeholder budget is the two sheets, and
the poses come along for free when the sheets are replaced.
