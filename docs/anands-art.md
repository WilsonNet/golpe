# Anands' art — the plan (start here)

**Status (2026-09-24): not started. This is the brief for the next session.**
Lia and Jeffs moved to Blender renders (`art/README.md`). Anands must **not**
go down that road as-is: her hand-drawn boards are the look the whole project
is chasing, and the renders — good as they are — are basic next to them. The
goal of this work is to make the *game* show her boards faithfully and give
her the clips she lacks, **without losing the boards' style**. Treat it as a
long job; do it in measured steps.

## What "her soul" is

The three boards (`unprocessed-sprites/anands-running.jpeg`,
`anands-misc.jpeg`, `anands-ultimate.jpeg` — 1024x1024, AI-generated pixel
art) are the reference. What makes them read as SNES art, and what any new or
repaired frame must keep:

- **A flat front elevation.** No camera tilt; limbs and weapons stay in the
  picture plane.
- **Flat fills with a shade band on the far edge.** Shapes are one colour,
  with the shadow hugging the side away from the light. They are not shaded
  like round 3D forms.
- **The face turned to the viewer** even when the body is side-on.
- **Chunky proportions.** Thick limbs, big boots and hands, a readable hand
  with its weapon.
- **A dark, colour-tinted outline** and a limited palette: purple hair, teal
  goggles, orange scarf, green top, brown trousers and pack, purple boots.
- **Big, readable key poses.** The run's extended stride, the gun braced at
  the hip, the dagger arm fully extended.

## How she ships today

`scripts/make-anands-art.py` cuts the boards into:

| File | Cells | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `anands.png` | 35 x 168x152 | 0-3 run right, 4 face-on, 5-8 run left, 9-10 idle, 11-14 gun hold/fire, 15-18 gun run, 19-22 stab, 23-28 shoryuken, 29-32 thrust windup/dash, 33-34 damage |
| `anands-roll.png` | 16 x 168x152 | the tumble — the face-on frame **rotated** (not drawn) |
| `anands-dragon.png` | 6 x 352x176 | the dragon-thrust ride |
| `anands-portrait.png` | 128x192 | the face-on frame, for the menus and the ultimate card |

The clip table is code: `HERO_CLIPS.anands` in `src/game/ecs/systems.ts`.
She is drawn at `48/152` through `SHEET_CELLS`, where the cell height is the
body height.

## What is wrong, measured

1. **The keying eats her art. This is the "skin looks bad in-game" bug.**
   The script removes the board by *colour*: beige panels, charcoal frames and
   pale-gold grid lines, each with a tolerance. Her skin, shirt, trousers and
   goggle lenses sit close to those colours, so they are deleted along with
   the board and the sky shows through her. The JPEG compression makes it
   worse. Measured on the shipped `anands.png`: on average **21% of the area
   inside her silhouette is transparent** (fully enclosed holes only, per
   cell 16–32%; the shoryuken cells are worst). Put the sheet on a solid green
   background to see it at a glance. There is also board debris in the cells:
   grid-line dashes at the top, floor lines, muzzle-flash fragments.
2. **Coverage is thin next to Lia and Jeffs.** No `__animStats` fallback is
   counted for her, because she is a strip hero and not a packed sheet, so
   nothing reports these gaps:
   - **launched, helpless, roll:** generated by rotating or tinting the
     face-on frame, not drawn.
   - **downed:** reuses a standing damage frame.
   - **hit poses:** rear-facing only.
   - **jump / fall:** none; she runs in the air.
   - **gun:** not aim-banded; the machine gun never points up or down.
   - **trap throw:** there is an item-use panel on the board, but no clip.
   - **ultimate:** no dragon transition, although the board has the
     neutral→dragon and run→dragon rows.
   - **short clips:** two-frame stab, one-frame thrust windup.
3. **The boards are not at native pixel resolution.** They are AI pixel art
   rendered at a non-integer scale (one "pixel" is several image pixels, with
   JPEG noise). The cutter resamples them to 168x152 cells, which bakes that
   softness in.

## Constraints

- **Her drawn frames stay authoritative.** Replace a drawn frame only when the
  user has seen and approved the replacement. A Blender render of her is a
  *reference*, never a ship candidate on its own.
- **The game's contracts still apply:**
  - the scale comes from the body box (`bodyH`), never the cell;
  - the cell centre is the collider centre, and the feet sit on the floor line;
  - clip names are `ClipName`s, and every clip is stored facing both ways;
  - `sheetDrawsBlade` stays false for her. The dagger is drawn by `MeleeFx`
    unless her own frames draw it; decide which per move, and never both.
- The boards themselves live in `unprocessed-sprites/`. Never overwrite them.

## Recommended order

1. **Measure first.** Build `scripts/anands-art-probe.py` or a `measure` step
   in `make-anands-art.py`, in the style of `make-hero-art.py`'s:
   - hole fraction inside the silhouette per cell (target ≈ 0; baseline mean
     21%);
   - debris pixel count outside the figure's main connected component
     (target 0);
   - figure height and feet line per cell (consistent across frames);
   - colour count against her palette.

   Numbers first, so every later step shows movement.
2. **Fix the extraction without redrawing anything.**
   - Remove the background by **flood fill from outside the figure** (panel
     colour connected to the cell border), not by colour anywhere. Interior
     pixels survive whatever their colour.
   - Drop components not connected to the figure.
   - Recover the **native pixel grid** (detect the board's pixel size and
     downsample by mode), then **quantize to her palette** so JPEG noise
     becomes clean pixel art.
   - Candidate tools for exactly this job (recover the true grid of AI pixel
     art, then snap and quantize), to evaluate side by side on one of her
     cells:
     - [proper-pixel-art](https://github.com/KennethJAllen/proper-pixel-art)
       (`pip install proper-pixel-art`);
     - [Retro Diffusion's pixel-art-fixer](https://github.com/Retro-Diffusion/pixel-art-fixer);
     - [Real-PixelArt](https://github.com/Bocchi-The-Glock/Real-PixelArt).

     Keep whichever scores best on the step-1 metrics and looks right to the
     user.

   Re-measure: holes → ~0, debris → 0. Then look at it in-game with the user.
3. **Move her to the packed-sheet format** (`<hero>.json` with `bodyH`,
   trimmed frames, clips in JSON, both facings, `PACKED_SHEETS`) so the atlas
   test and `art-probe.ts --hero=anands` cover her and fallbacks get counted.
   Her clip table moves out of code in the process.
4. **Fill the gaps one clip at a time, in her style.** In priority order:
   1. real launched / downed / helpless poses, and front-facing hit poses;
   2. jump / fall;
   3. a drawn roll;
   4. an aim-banded machine gun;
   5. the trap throw;
   6. the dragon transition, already on the board: wire it in as the cast.

   Sources, best first:
   1. **panels already on the boards** that are not cut yet (item use,
      victory, the ultimate transitions, the contextual idles);
   2. **new boards generated the same way the originals were** (Gemini /
      Nano Banana — see Decisions): the same character, style and panel
      layout, run through the same extraction;
   3. **paint-overs** in a pixel editor (LibreSprite is free; Aseprite is the
      standard), optionally over a Blender pose from `sprite_rig.py` used
      only as a skeleton guide.
5. After each step: `make-anands-art.py` measure, the atlas test,
   `art-probe --hero=anands`, and a side-by-side against the board shown to
   the user.

## Decisions (from the user, 2026-09-24)

- **More boards: yes.** The originals were made with Gemini ("Nano Banana")
  and it got the look right. New clips should come from **new boards
  generated the same way**: same character, same style, same panel layout.
  Then they go through the same extraction. This is the preferred source for
  every missing clip (step 4). Before generating anything, write the prompt
  down in this doc, reusing the originals' wording where it is known, so
  every board is asked for the same way.
- **Crisp, like the spritesheet: yes.** The target is the boards as they
  look: clean pixel art at its true resolution, with hard edges and flat
  colours. It is not a resampled, softened copy. Recovering the native pixel
  grid and quantizing to her palette (step 2) is in scope.
- **The dagger goes into her frames.** In the future her art draws the
  dagger, like Lia's and Jeffs' swords, and `MeleeFx` keeps only the trail
  (`sheetDrawsBlade`). Every stab, thrust and shoryuken frame then needs the
  dagger drawn at the right angle, so board prompts for her moves should ask
  for the dagger visible in hand.
